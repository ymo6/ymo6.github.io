About Me

I am a Computational Research Associate in the Department of Genetics at Stanford University, where I work with Professor Jesse Engreitz. My research focuses on dissecting gene regulatory mechanisms by combining CRISPR-based perturbation screens, single-cell genomics and statistical inference methods, and agentic system interpretation.

Prior to Stanford, I earned my M.S. in Biomedical Engineering and dual B.S. degrees in Computer Science and Chemical & Biomolecular Engineering from Johns Hopkins University, where I worked with Professor Patrick Cahan on information-theoretic inference of dynamic gene regulatory networks from single-nucleus multi-omics data.

Research Vision

CRISPR screens now perturb thousands of genes across millions of single cells — but turning that data into mechanism is still slow, manual, and hard to reproduce. The signal is further limited by noise in the sequencing technology itself: much of what we measure reflects the experiment rather than the biology.

My goal is to close that gap by building predictive models of biology that stay robust against the covariates introduced during the experiment and at sequencing time — and then, with the help of agentic AI, extending how much of the result a person can actually interpret. That means GPU-native factorization methods (PerturbNMF), information-theoretic network inference that reaches down to individual regulatory elements (CRE-Finder), and agentic systems that ground the resulting programs in the literature automatically (AGeneTic).

Selected Projects

PerturbNMF

GPU · Perturb-seq · gene programs

An end-to-end pipeline porting consensus NMF to GPU with a HALS solver. Matches CPU cNMF on biological accuracy while cutting compute time up to 20×, making consortium-scale gene program analysis practical. Applied to hiPSC-to-endothelial Perturb-seq, it surfaced a previously unreported role for Hippo signaling in cell fate.

Code · Manuscript

CRE-Finder

Information theory · multi-omics · GRN inference

An information-theoretic algorithm that reconstructs dynamic gene regulatory networks and predicts distal cis-regulatory elements from single-nucleus multi-omics data — connecting regulatory elements to the genes they control across developmental time.

First-author manuscript in preparation

AGeneTic

Agentic AI · LLMs · literature grounding

An LLM-based agentic framework for automated gene set analysis that annotates discovered gene programs against the primary literature using PaperQA2 — turning an unnamed factor into a citation-backed hypothesis. Second Place, UCSF QBI Hackathon 2026.

In active development

Research Interests

My work sits at the intersection of functional genomics, machine learning, and software engineering:

Gene Program Discovery at Scale: matrix factorization and dimensionality reduction for Perturb-seq, GPU-accelerated solvers, consensus and stability analysis across single-cell modalities

Gene Regulatory Network Inference: information-theoretic reconstruction of dynamic GRNs, prediction of distal cis-regulatory elements from single-nucleus multi-omics data

Agentic AI for Biological Interpretation: LLM-based frameworks for automated gene set analysis and literature-grounded annotation of gene programs
